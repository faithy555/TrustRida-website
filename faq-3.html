Questions before you pay

The real questions a first-time buyer asks before trusting a rider they've never met.

Trust & Identity

Trustrida shows a photo, government or company ID, and bike plate number tied to that specific delivery — not a general profile. Match the face and plate before you open your door.
IDs are checked against the rider's registered details before a link goes live, so a mismatched or fake ID doesn't generate a valid page.
Every real link opens live on Trustrida's site with current rider status — a screenshot can't do that. If it's not opening as a live page, don't trust it.

Payment

No. You check the verification page first — photo, ID, plate, phone — before any money moves.
Trustrida confirms the rider is real and dispatched; it's not a payment escrow, so agree payment terms (part-payment, pay-on-arrival) directly with the seller.
Transfer receipts only prove money moved — they say nothing about who's bringing your goods. Trustrida verifies the person, not the payment.

Using the Link

That's a red flag — a real Trustrida page always loads. Don't proceed with payment until you get a working link.
Sellers can assign different riders per order, so always check the link for that specific delivery — don't assume it's the same rider as last time.

Why This Over Alternatives

Those apps verify riders for their own platform but don't give an independent seller a shareable proof link for buyers to check beforehand — Trustrida is built for that gap.
You're not really "going first" — you're verifying the rider before payment, which is the whole point: proof before trust, not trust before proof.
